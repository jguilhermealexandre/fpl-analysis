What it does

How a run gets rated

Both directions

A fixture is easy for an attacker and hard for a defender at the same time. Every club is rated for what it creates and what it concedes, so a run is read differently depending on who you own.

Home and away are different teams

Venue is never averaged away. A side that is miserly at home and porous on the road is exactly the kind of club a flat difficulty rating gets wrong.

Clean sheet odds, not vibes

The chance of a clean sheet is the zero bucket of a Poisson distribution at that match\u2019s expected goals against — so it moves with the fixture rather than with reputation.

The next five, at a glance

Plan a wildcard or a double-up around the schedule rather than around last weekend, with the whole run visible for every club at once.

Worth knowing

Where fixture-led transfers go wrong

Buying the badge

A big club with a hard run is still a hard run. The rating does not care who the shirt belongs to.

Reading colours

Three green squares can hide one very hard away trip. The number behind the colour is what the projection uses.

Forgetting rotation

An easy run is worth nothing to a player who will be rested for it. Fixture ratings live beside minutes for that reason.
