How prices actually move

A player's price rises when enough managers buy him relative to how many already own him. That last clause is what most price-watch coverage misses: a player owned by 300,000 managers needs a fraction of the buys that a player owned by four million does. Raw transfer counts therefore mislead in both directions, and the useful figure is net transfers divided by the existing ownership base.

Squad value risk

Squad value is not a scoreboard. It is a constraint that decides which squads you can build later in the season, and it leaks quietly rather than dramatically. The mechanism is worth stating precisely, because it is where most of the confusion lives.

When a player's price falls by a tenth, you lose a tenth of squad value immediately. When it rises by a tenth, you only bank half of the rise — FPL charges 50% sell-on tax, rounded down. So a player bought at £7.0m who climbs to £7.4m sells for £7.2m, not £7.4m. The asymmetry means falls cost you more than rises earn you, and it is why avoiding falls beats chasing rises as a strategy.

The four rules that actually matter

  • Never make a transfer to catch a price rise. A tenth of a million is worth roughly nothing; a wasted transfer is worth four points.
  • Never hold a player you have decided to sell. The fall is already priced in by the time you notice it, and waiting only deepens it.
  • Buy early in the week when you are confident, late when you are not. Information arrives across the week — press conferences, training reports. Trading a tenth for a team-news update is a good trade.
  • Ignore value entirely until the second half of the season. It is a constraint that binds late, not a score that runs throughout.

Risk check

FPL does not publish its price-change thresholds, and never has. The pressure column above is a model built from net transfers against the ownership base, not the real number, and it will be wrong at the margins. Nobody outside the game's developers knows the actual formula; anyone claiming otherwise has reverse-engineered an approximation too.
